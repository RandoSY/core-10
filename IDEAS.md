# CORE 10 — Idea Record

## Problem

Science education is often split between abstract instruction and laboratory activities that are expensive, inconvenient, disconnected, or dependent on specialized equipment. Students can complete labs without developing a coherent sense of measurement, model building, energy, uncertainty, or practical judgment.

## Central idea

Build a compact science architecture from inexpensive, repeatable experiences in which learners first encounter a phenomenon, construct or calibrate measurement, gather evidence, model the relationship, and only then compress the experience into formal mathematics.

## Governing principles

- **Experience Before Equation** — encounter and measure before formal compression.
- **Need Before Technology** — introduce tools because the investigation requires them.
- **Not Talk, Do** — the learner must leave able to act, measure, test, and explain.
- **How much? / Enough?** — quantify reality, then judge sufficiency against a purpose or threshold.

## Canonical sibling branches

### Kitchen Inquiry Lab
Treat ordinary household processes as rigorous experimental systems. Heating, cooling, evaporation, appliances, food, mass change, and timing become accessible physical science.

### One Cup Chemistry
Use one inexpensive vessel and a small sensor/tool set to build a connected chemistry sequence. Continuity of apparatus reduces management overhead and makes evidence and reasoning central.

### NOTHING IS FREE / Personal Energy Laboratory
Trace energy through heating, storage, motion, batteries, food, machines, and living systems. Connect joules and watts to watt-hours, kilowatt-hours, and real cost.

## Measurement ladder

The learner should experience measurement as a human construction:

1. invent/make a ruler or unit;
2. calibrate and compare;
3. connect length, area, volume, mass, and water standards;
4. construct time measurement;
5. study motion and periodic phenomena;
6. graph and model relationships;
7. move toward calculators, computation, and AI-assisted interpretation.

## Fidelity mirrors

A durable relationship should be encountered through multiple representations:

- physical phenomenon;
- paper/symbolic mathematics;
- analog/electrical embodiment where useful;
- digital simulation/computation.

The learning goal is translation among representations, not loyalty to one representation.

## Required energy-cost sequence

Microwave water heating is a first-class investigation. Learners distinguish electrical input from measured useful thermal energy (`Q = mcΔT`) and follow the unit chain **joules → watts → Wh → kWh → dollars**. A real electric bill supplies the cost context, including fixed charges and effective all-in cost where appropriate.

## Why it matters

CORE 10 aims to preserve scientific agency under constrained circumstances. Its experiments are intended to remain teachable when budgets, hardware platforms, software ecosystems, or classroom infrastructure change.

## Distinctive contribution

The contribution is not a numbered list of ten topics. It is the architecture that makes a small physical toolkit carry a much larger body of scientific reasoning.

## Reconstruction path

Start with measurement and one ordinary phenomenon. Require prediction, physical evidence, explicit units, uncertainty/limitations, a model, and an explanation. Revisit the same relationship through multiple representations and eventually ask both: **How much?** and **Is that enough for the purpose?**
