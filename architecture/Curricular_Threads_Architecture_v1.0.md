# Curricular Threads Architecture

**Version:** proposal v1.0, September 2026  
**Recovery provenance:** derived from `Curricular_Threads_Architecture_Proposal.pdf` in the Intellectual Estate Library.  
**Purpose:** preserve the curriculum's enduring architecture separately from replaceable boards, apps, sensors, transports, and AI models.

## Design proposition

Preserve direct contact with physical reality where it matters; move reasoning, reusable data, simulation, scenario management, and preserved experiment records to the web wherever possible; use AI to connect, adapt, critique, and administer the learning experience.

The legacy goal is not a collection of activities. It is a way of understanding and administering the physical conditions of life.

## Architecture at a glance

**One root. Three disciplinary branches. Two cross-cutting threads. One integrative application.**

### Root — CORE 10

CORE 10 owns the common language of inquiry:

- measurement;
- quantity;
- uncertainty;
- proportionality;
- graphs;
- conservation;
- models;
- experimental reasoning.

Later branches assume this foundation rather than restarting it.

Memory line:

`Measure • Observe • Quantify • Model • Conserve • Transform • Predict • Test`

### Three sibling branches

1. **Kitchen Inquiry Lab** — everyday physics and household energy.
2. **One Cup Chemistry** — matter, solutions and change.
3. **NOTHING IS FREE (NIF)** — energy, work, storage and cost.

These branches remain siblings with distinct intellectual ownership. They are not collapsed into one undifferentiated sequence.

### Cross-cutting threads

- **Biology / living systems** supplies the context of organisms whose material and energy requirements matter.
- **SDL / Software-Defined Laboratory** supplies sensing, logging, preservation, visualization, experiment adaptation, capability description and AI-facing infrastructure.

### Integrative application

**Personal Energy Laboratory** is the synthesis environment. It should contain almost no introductory science of its own. Its job is to require prior knowledge under finite-resource constraints.

Central question:

> How do I maintain viable human life when energy is finite?

## The progression

`Encounter → Measure → Explain → Reuse → Integrate → Decide`

This progression is the principal anti-duplication mechanism. A phenomenon may return, but its intellectual job must change.

## Reinforcement without duplication

Repeat a concept when useful. Do not repeat an experiment unless the second encounter produces a genuinely new intellectual result.

### CANONICAL

The definitive first physical encounter with a phenomenon or measurement relationship.

Test: would later branches lose something essential if this experience did not exist?

### REVISIT

A return to the same phenomenon or preserved dataset with a new question, model, scale, or decision.

Test: what can the learner now infer or decide that was impossible before?

### SYNTHESIS

A task requiring knowledge from multiple earlier threads under realistic constraints.

Test: does successful completion require retrieval and integration rather than re-teaching?

### Administrative rule

If two activities use the same apparatus, the same measurement, the same graph, and reach the same conclusion, merge them or explicitly reuse the earlier experiment.

## Example — one water-heating event, many curricular lives

- **CORE 10:** mass, temperature, time, measurement quality and graphing.
- **Kitchen Inquiry:** electrical input versus thermal output, microwave energy, kWh and cost.
- **One Cup Chemistry:** water or brine becomes the material context for solution/thermal-chemistry questions; thermometer reading is no longer the lesson.
- **NIF:** reuse the earlier measured record to ask conversion-loss and storage-burden questions.
- **Personal Energy Laboratory:** decide whether and when to heat water given generation forecast, battery reserve, competing loads and human need.

The physical event need not be repeated merely to give every curricular branch its own copy.

## Persistent experiment record

Every canonical experiment should leave a reusable digital record containing:

- raw measurements;
- apparatus/context metadata;
- calibration information;
- uncertainty notes;
- graph(s);
- learner interpretation;
- conclusions;
- later AI annotations where useful.

Later activities should be able to reopen that record and ask a new question.

## Branch ownership

### CORE 10 — scientific foundation

Canonical experiences include:

- DIY ruler / dimensional measurement;
- π investigation;
- graduated volume;
- water mass-volume relationship;
- balance / equilibrium;
- Galileo timing / ramp;
- pendulum;
- basic electrical measurement;
- basic thermal measurement;
- conservation exercises.

### Kitchen Inquiry Lab — physics of ordinary living

Primary ownership includes:

- microwave water heating;
- cooling curves;
- insulation comparison;
- refrigerator duty cycle;
- cooking-method comparison;
- appliance power survey;
- real electric bill;
- hot-water use.

Microwave heating is therefore not merely a thermal demonstration. It owns the chain from electrical input to useful thermal energy to kWh and cost.

### One Cup Chemistry — matter, solutions and change

Primary ownership includes:

- concentration series;
- conductivity and ions;
- solubility / saturation;
- brine cooling and freezing;
- heat of dissolution;
- crystallization;
- acid-base indication;
- electrochemical cells;
- Beer-Lambert / colorimetry;
- biological chemistry anchor(s).

### NOTHING IS FREE — energy accounting

Primary ownership includes:

- measured device charging in Wh;
- rechargeable-battery charge/discharge;
- usable capacity and reserve;
- input/output energy loss;
- small solar generation;
- solar-to-battery chain;
- electrical-to-mechanical work;
- autonomous robot energy use;
- route/runtime optimization;
- Wh/kWh economics and trade-offs.

NIF should import the Kitchen water-heating record rather than re-run it merely to introduce energy accounting.

## Biology / living systems as a cross-cutting thread

Initial anchors include:

- photosynthesis and biomass;
- respiration and metabolism;
- human thermoregulation;
- hydration and osmosis;
- food energy and preservation;
- growth and ecological exchange.

The oak-tree question is an exemplar because it forces matter, atmosphere, biology, chemistry and energy into one coherent account.

## SDL as cross-cutting experimental infrastructure

SDL is not another subject. It follows the learner from a cheap USB-connected instrument to networked and remotely programmable systems without changing the underlying inquiry pattern.

Its functions are:

- **sense / acquire** — make quantities inspectable;
- **log / preserve** — give experiments reusable digital lives;
- **visualize** — reveal patterns and support models;
- **describe capabilities** — let software and AI know what apparatus can actually do;
- **interact with AI** — support evidence-grounded experimental reasoning;
- **adapt experiments** — change procedure, sampling, prompting or analysis when useful;
- **challenge assumptions** — identify weak experimental or explanatory premises.

The architectural goal is that a very inexpensive instrument can participate in the same intellectual structure as a sophisticated laboratory.

## Personal Energy Laboratory

This is where scientific understanding becomes agency.

It recruits prior knowledge to answer practical requirements such as:

- stay warm;
- stay cool;
- stay transported;
- stay fed;
- stay hydrated;
- stay powered.

A synthesis scenario may supply finite solar generation, a partially charged battery, heating and refrigeration needs, transportation requirements and water-heating demand. The learner is not asked to rediscover watts, heat capacity or battery capacity. The learner must make and defend a plan.

The curriculum's final purpose is not “learn solar.” It is to understand matter, energy, living systems, measurement and economics well enough to administer the conditions of life under constraint.

## Web virtualization

Virtualize aggressively, but not indiscriminately.

### Tier A — fully web-native

Good candidates include:

- explanations and interactive models;
- graphing and re-analysis of preserved authentic datasets;
- energy-budget scenarios;
- solar/weather/load simulations;
- battery state-of-charge planning;
- household dispatch problems;
- AI tutoring and critique;
- experiment design and fault diagnosis using device descriptions and archived data.

### Tier B — web-first, hardware optional

Examples include solar-panel behavior, battery cycling, heating/cooling, conductivity/solution chemistry and robot energy use. Archived measured datasets and models can provide access, while physical work adds intermittency, drift, contamination, lag, friction, voltage sag and other realities.

### Tier C — deliberate physical anchors

Keep a small number of cheap experiences because simulation tends to hide important realities:

- measurement and calibration;
- mass / volume / density;
- heat and cooling;
- preparing a solution;
- simple circuits and sensors;
- motion / robot work.

The target is not “no hardware.” It is:

> the minimum physical contact needed to make the virtual and AI layers trustworthy and meaningful.

## Access ladder

- **Browser only:** concepts, simulations, authentic datasets, scenarios and AI interaction.
- **Browser + borrowed/shared kit:** occasional canonical anchors and uploaded experiment records.
- **Personal low-cost kit:** local canonical experiments with reusable hardware.
- **Networked SDL laboratory:** AI/device capability discovery and adaptive experimentation.

Hardware adds reality; it should not be the gate that unlocks the curriculum.

## AI and the adaptive laboratory

The long-lived asset is not a fixed app. It is a machine-readable curriculum plus machine-readable experimental capability.

AI roles may include:

- tutor;
- planner;
- instrument collaborator;
- data analyst;
- critic;
- safety/constraint checker;
- scenario manager;
- archivist.

The experiment itself should be describable independently of a particular board or app, including:

- question;
- required observables;
- acceptable instruments;
- safety constraints;
- expected reasoning;
- reusable outputs;
- downstream curricular uses.

This is how future web interfaces, kits and AI systems can change without destroying the curriculum.

## Preservation and longevity

### Preserve as enduring assets

- curricular hierarchy and ownership;
- canonical experiment definitions;
- scientific models and concepts;
- safety constraints and valid ranges;
- authentic datasets and experiment records;
- machine-readable capability descriptions;
- scenario libraries and synthesis problems;
- plain-language learner explanations.

### Allow to evolve

- microcontroller boards;
- sensors/vendors;
- USB/BLE/Wi-Fi/MQTT transport choices;
- dashboard frameworks;
- AI model/provider;
- programming language/runtime;
- hosting platform;
- packaging and branding.

### Archive design rules

Prefer ordinary portable forms such as CSV/JSON, Markdown/HTML, images and simple schemas. Browser counterparts should use authentic preserved data. Simulations should disclose what is modeled versus measured. Hardware implementations should be substitutable. Device descriptions should remain human-readable as well as AI-readable. AI must be replaceable.

Preserve the learner's evidence and reasoning, not only final answers.

## Minimal complete implementation

1. Inventory existing CORE 10, Kitchen Inquiry, One Cup and NIF activities.
2. Tag each as CANONICAL, REVISIT, SYNTHESIS or REDUNDANT.
3. Assign primary ownership.
4. Preserve one authentic dataset plus metadata/uncertainty for each canonical experiment.
5. Create a browser twin that can explain, display and re-analyze the evidence.
6. Add AI that can explain, critique, request missing evidence and adapt follow-up work.
7. Demonstrate one Personal Energy scenario that reuses earlier measurements and requires finite-resource decisions.

Minimal demonstration loop:

`physical reality → preserved evidence → web reasoning → AI adaptation → practical decision`

## Durability criterion

The curriculum should survive the disappearance of any particular board, app, AI model or classroom.

What must remain is:

- the intellectual architecture;
- the experimental evidence;
- the learner's ability to reason from physical reality.

**UNDERSTAND MATTER • UNDERSTAND ENERGY • UNDERSTAND LIFE**  
**MEASURE THEM • CONNECT THEM • USE THEM TO REMAIN CAPABLE**
