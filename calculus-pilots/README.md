# Ten Problems That Teach Calculus — Classroom pilots

Two pilot lessons follow the CORE 10 principle **Experience Before Equation**. Learners make a decision first, explain what they need, and only then attach the formal calculus idea to a job it does.

- **[Student handouts](student-pilot-handouts.pdf)** — Four US Letter pages. Print the A and B sheets separately so the first experience isn't spoiled.
- **[Teacher guide](teacher-pilot-guide.pdf)** — Four US Letter pages with timing, release instructions, discussion prompts, worked keys, transfer checks, and a small pilot rubric. Keep the late practice data teacher-side until students commit to a prediction.
- **[Download both PDFs as a ZIP](calculus-classroom-pilots.zip)**.

## The two lessons

| Lesson | What students decide | Independent transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **06 · Cart on a track** | Why two clients asking how far the cart went need different reports. | Use a battery log to distinguish net charge from total charge moved. |
| **09 · Kitchen Inquiry Lab** | How to predict a target temperature and decide whether the prediction was good enough using readings held back during fitting. | Predict a humidity crossing and choose a later reading that could test it. |

Lesson 09 works with supervised live measurements or a staged, explicitly synthetic practice record. The two cases are ready to pilot but have not been classroom-tested. Use the worked-solutions PDF and Python programs from the original calculus pack as follow-through, not as instructions to hand out before the investigation.
