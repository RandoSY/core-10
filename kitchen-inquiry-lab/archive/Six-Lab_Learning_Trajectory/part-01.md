| **⚠️ Safety:** Keep electronics dry; wipe spills immediately. Avoid dripping water onto scales. |

**📋 Procedure**

**1.** Start a condition log: room temperature, location, instrument
model, any unusual factors (drafts, sun, wet counter).

**2.** Scale repeatability test: weigh the same object 10 times. Record
each reading.

**3.** Cup claim test: fill to a marked volume (e.g., 200 mL). Weigh the
water (tare the cup first).

**4.** Thermometer check: place probe in a well-stirred ice slurry (tip
submerged, not touching container). Record stabilised reading.

**5.** Tag anomalies as Random (scatter), Systematic (bias), or Blunder
(procedural mistake).

<td><p><strong>Purpose</strong></p>
<p>Students create a calibration curve for their container and learn why
fitted models beat single readings.</p></td>
<td></td>
<td><p><strong>Core Measurement Idea</strong></p>
<p>Density links mass and volume. A line fit over multiple points
exposes bias and improves accuracy beyond any single reading.</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 100%" />

<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><p><strong>The Key Insight</strong></p>
<p>For water at 20°C: 1 mL ≡ 1 g (density ≈ 0.998 g/mL). Therefore mass
of water IS the reference — and the cup graduation is the thing being
tested. If the slope of your calibration line deviates from 1.00, the
cup has systematic bias.</p></td>
</tr>

| **⚠️ Safety:** Wipe spills immediately to prevent slipping and to protect scales. |

**📋 Procedure**

**1.** Log conditions: water temperature, container type, scale
resolution.

**2.** Tare container on the scale.

**3.** Add water to 5 volume marks (100, 200, 300, 400, 500 mL). At each
mark, record mass.

**4.** Optional: repeat with warmer water (record temperature).

**5.** If any point looks off, do one repeat measurement before moving
on.
