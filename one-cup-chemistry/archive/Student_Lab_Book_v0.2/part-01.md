# How to Use This Book

**1. Predict before measuring** - Write what you think will happen and
why. A prediction can be wrong and still be scientifically useful.

**2. Preserve raw data** - Record exactly what the instrument reports.
Never “clean up” raw measurements to make a graph prettier.

**3. Separate evidence types** - Label statements as measured,
calculated, simulated, inferred, or external evidence.

**4. Use equations after the phenomenon** - Equations are tools for
compressing and testing observations, not substitutes for observing.

**5. Challenge explanations** - Every guide asks what assumption could
be wrong and what evidence would change your mind.

**6. Treat AI as a reasoning participant** - AI may predict, critique,
calculate, or explain. Its answers are claims to be checked against the
physical system.

## Laboratory Rules

> **•** Wear eye protection whenever the guide or teacher requires it.
>
> **•** Nothing used in this laboratory is tasted or returned to food
> use.
>
> **•** Keep liquids away from the UNO, USB cable, computer, and power
> connections.
>
> **•** Use only low-voltage, current-limited electronics around
> solutions. Never connect mains electricity to electrodes.
>
> **•** Commercial deicers are laboratory chemicals here; follow the
> product label and SDS.
>
> **•** CaCl2 can heat strongly when dissolving. Add it gradually to
> cool/room-temperature water in an open vessel.
>
> **•** Do not seal gas-producing reactions.
>
> **•** Report spills, broken equipment, unexpected heating, or unusual
> odors immediately.

# Your Evidence Vocabulary

| **MEASURED**          | Direct output from an instrument or direct observation.                                      |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **CALCULATED**        | Produced from measured values using an explicit equation.                                    |
| **SIMULATED**         | Produced by a computational model.                                                           |
| **INFERRED**          | An explanation or conclusion supported by evidence.                                          |
| **EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** | A literature value, SDS, database structure, or other source outside this class measurement. |

**Rule:** never let a calculation or AI explanation silently become
“what we observed.”

## Procedure

**1.** Zero/tare the balance with the empty cup.

**2.** Add a nominal 100 mL water by cylinder; record actual indicated
volume.

**3.** Measure mass and temperature.

**4.** Repeat five times, emptying/drying as needed.

**5.** Compare class values and calculate mean, range, and optional
standard deviation.

## Theory - after the evidence

• A measurement is a number plus a unit plus an uncertainty.

• Random variation broadens repeated values; systematic error shifts
them together.

• Density later links mass and volume, but first treat each instrument
as an independent witness.
